## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** "I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything. There's 15,000 titles but nothing I actually want. I ended up going back to a DVD." (UXR-01, Priya, 34)
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Content searched then played fell from 41% to 34% (▼7 pts) over six months - the closest funnel metric to a search ending in zero watches. Corroborating but distinct: Title Detail → Start Playing conversion fell from 48% to 29%, the steepest pre-play drop in the funnel.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Role: 14-year, heavy-usage subscriber (Power User segment - 22% of base, highest LTV at $14.20, 4.8 sessions/week, already 58% curated-content consumption). 
Goal: spend limited evening time watching, not searching. 
Friction confirmed: the pre-play search-to-nothing failure is real and shows up in funnel data, but it does not register as segment-level churn risk - Power Users are flagged "N/A, low churn anyway" under Spotlight exposure. The pain is confirmed as an engagement/session-quality problem, not a churn problem, for this segment.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** High-usage subscribers who already value curated content still hit dead-end searches that end in no watch, eroding session depth and engagement even though it isn't (yet) visible as subscription churn.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behavior change: fewer dead-end searches, more searches converting to a play, and more sessions crossing the 30-minute engagement threshold. This maps to engagement depth and LTV protection within an already-low-churn, high-value segment, rather than to churn reduction directly - the business case here is defending session quality and usage frequency in the segment that drives the most revenue per user, not preventing cancellations.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Content-searched-then-played rate for Power Users, recovering toward or above the 41% prior baseline (from current 34%).
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Power User sessions/week (currently 4.8×) must not decline - curation narrowing the discovery surface should not come at the cost of the browsing frequency that already defines this segment's engagement.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** One full retention/engagement cycle (90 days, matching the cohort cadence in Snapshot 2) tracked against the searched-then-played metric specifically for Power Users; proceed/scale at a ≥5 pt recovery, pivot toward a Wanderer-focused variant if flat, kill if it declines further or if guardrail sessions/week drops.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on Priya's reported dead-end search pattern (UXR-01) and the funnel data showing content-searched-then-played fell from 41% to 34%, I believe that solving pre-play discovery failure for Power User subscribers like Priya will result in deeper, more consistently rewarding sessions, as measured by a 5+ point recovery in content-searched-then-played rate (34% toward the 41% baseline) within 90 days. I will protect Power User sessions/week (currently 4.8×) so that narrowing the discovery surface doesn't suppress the browsing behavior that already defines this segment. I will make a go/no-go decision after one full 90-day cohort cycle, scaling on a ≥5 pt recovery, pivoting if flat, and killing if sessions/week declines.
